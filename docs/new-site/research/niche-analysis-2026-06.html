Bottom line: the category is commoditized on features and splits into enterprise generalists (Birdeye, Podium, Broadly), affordable specialists (NiceJob, GatherUp, PulseM), and the field-service CRMs' own built-in tools (Housecall Pro; ServiceTitan via Birdeye). Job-triggered SMS+email requests, CRM integrations, AI replies, widgets, and social posting are table stakes. The real openings are price & contract terms, vertical focus, platform breadth vs the CRM built-ins, and compliance.

Table stakes vs differentiators

Table stakes — must match
  • Job-completion SMS + email review requests
  • CRM / field-service integrations
  • Multi-site review monitoring & alerts
  • AI-generated review replies
  • Website review widgets
  • Social auto-posting, QR codes
Differentiators — where to win
  • Technician / employee leaderboards
  • Map-pack rank tracking (rank movement, not just counts)
  • Missed-call text-back
  • Broad multi-platform routing (Google + Facebook + Yelp + Angi) vs the CRMs' Google/Facebook-only

Pricing reality

  • Enterprise: $299–$599+/mo per location, annual contracts, big implementation fees.
  • Sweet spot for small shops: ~$75–$150/mo, month-to-month, no contract (NiceJob, GatherUp own this).
  • Per-technician pricing (PulseM) punishes growth — a 12-tech shop pays ~$1,188/mo. A flat price is a clean attack angle.

Compliance — do's & don'ts

Don't
  • No review gating — routing unhappy customers to a private form is banned by Google & the FTC (FTC Final Rule, Oct 2024; Google removed 292M+ violating reviews in 2025). Competitors' funnels flirt with this.
  • No paying for / incentivizing reviews.
  • No fake reviews or testimonials (FTC).
Do
  • SMS opt-out (TCPA, Apr 11 2025): honor STOP/UNSUBSCRIBE/etc. within 10 business days; applies to calls + texts.
  • Register A2P 10DLC + automatic STOP handling & suppression.
  • Ask every customer (not just the happy ones) and let reviews land publicly.

What TableTurnerr should do to win

  1. Own "home-services only." Nobody is exclusively trades — most are horizontal or merely lean in.
  2. Compete on price & terms. Transparent, month-to-month, no contract, flat ~$99–$149 (not per-tech, not enterprise per-location). Publish pricing.
  3. Beat the CRM built-ins on platform breadth. "Housecall Pro only posts to Google & Facebook — we do Google, Facebook, Yelp and Angi." (Verified gap.)
  4. Make compliance a feature. "We never hide bad reviews — Google and the FTC ban that. Fully compliant, A2P-registered SMS." Differentiates from gating funnels + builds trust.
  5. Lean into rare differentiators. Technician leaderboards + map-pack rank tracking + missed-call text-back.
  6. Run the niche's GTM playbook. "X alternative" comparison pages, a free review-score grader lead magnet, and CRM marketplace listings.
  7. Integrate with the CRMs, don't fight them. Position as the multi-platform layer on top of Jobber/Housecall Pro/ServiceTitan.
