New-Site AuditJune 26, 2026

The new site is well-built. It isn't launch-complete yet.

A full read of the review-automation site — architecture, technical SEO, performance, content, conversion, compliance and backend — with the exact gaps between "looks done" and "ready to take money."

The build quality is genuinely strong. The information architecture is clean, the per-trade and per-city copy is hand-written (not find-and-replace), the metadata and JSON-LD are correct for review automation, security headers and 301s are in place, and assets are light. As an asset, this is a B+ site.

What holds it back from launch is everything that turns a visitor into a paying customer and keeps it legal: the signup flow is a manual lead form with no trial provisioning or billing; the free grader lead-magnet and the blog are empty; there are no competitor "alternative" pages; and the legal/compliance layer has real holes — no SMS/TCPA language, an unverified "4×" stat, and a Terms page that still describes the old agency services and contradicts the 90-day guarantee. Plus old restaurant/med-spa code still lingers backstage.

None of these are rebuilds. They're finish-the-job work on a foundation that's already done.

4 gaps
Stand between this and launch: self-serve conversion, the lead-magnet + content engine, compliance/legal, and brand cleanup

The new TableTurnerr site scores B- (72/100) on launch readiness — the kind of split where the asset is excellent and the go-to-market layer is unfinished. The site, content, and technical SEO are B+/A- work; conversion, compliance, and brand cleanup are C/D.

Top 5 findings:

1. The funnel doesn't close. Every CTA points at /signup or /contact, and both are the same manual lead form writing to contact_leads. There's no automated trial provisioning, no Stripe/billing, and no credentials email — the success message promises a trial that nothing actually creates.

2. The lead-magnet and content engine are empty. The free review-score grader (the niche's standard lead magnet) doesn't exist, the blog ships with zero live posts, and there are no "X alternative" comparison pages — the three highest-ROI organic plays in this niche.

3. The legal/compliance layer has real holes. No SMS/TCPA/A2P language anywhere for an SMS product; the homepage "4× more reviews" stat is unverified (FTC risk); the Terms page still describes the old agency services (website design, SEO, ads) and its "all payments non-refundable" clause contradicts the site-wide "90 days or next month free" guarantee.

4. The no-gating compliance angle is a wasted differentiator. The product correctly does not gate reviews — but the site never says so, leaving a strong, on-trend selling point on the table.

5. Old-brand code lingers backstage. The public marketing nav is clean, but constants.ts (SERVICES/FAQ/INDUSTRY_LINKS), the old layout/Navbar+Footer, and components/sections/* (ReviewCalculator, OurWork, ContactForm) still carry restaurant/med-spa copy and feed the portal + blog imports.

The good news: the four highest-leverage fixes are execution on an existing foundation, not new architecture.

One-liner: this is a "ship the funnel and finish the legal layer" problem, not a "build the site" problem. The site is the easy 80% that's done; the gaps are the 20% that actually converts and protects revenue.

  • "4× more reviews" stat is unverified (FTC §5 risk). Requalify as "typical customers see…" + cite.
  • Testimonials unattributed — names/locations but no verifiable link.
  • Terms describe the OLD agency (website/SEO/ads), not review automation.
  • Refund clause contradicts the guarantee — Terms say "non-refundable"; site says "90 days or next month free."
  • No-gating angle not marketed — a strong differentiator left unused.
  • Blog empty — zero TOFU/SEO content.
Compliance is the real launch blocker here. An SMS-based product with no opt-in/opt-out disclosure, an unverifiable hero stat, and a Terms page that both describes the wrong product and contradicts its own guarantee is exposed on three fronts (TCPA, FTC, and contract). Fix these before any paid traffic.
