## 0. How to read this report (plain English)

A few terms repeat throughout. Here's what they mean in normal language:

| Term | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| **Organic traffic** | Free visitors who find a site through Google (not ads). More organic traffic = Google trusts them = they get free leads every month. |
| **Ranking keywords / "queries"** | The number of different Google searches a site shows up for. If a site "ranks for 50,000 keywords," it appears in the results for 50,000 different searches. More = wider net = more ways to be found. |
| **KD (Keyword Difficulty)** | How hard it is to reach page 1 for a search, scored 0–100. **Under ~30 = realistic for us. 60+ = a fight against giants.** We chase low-KD terms. |
| **SERP** | The Google results page for a search. "We rank in the SERP" = we show up on that page. |
| **Programmatic / city pages** | One page template multiplied across many cities, e.g. "Restaurant Website Design in **Houston**", "...in **Dallas**", etc. One design → hundreds of pages, each catching local searches. |
| **Lead magnet** | A free thing (a tool, an audit, a checklist) that gets a business owner to hand over their email. We then sell to them. |
| **Backlinks / referring domains** | Other websites linking to you. Google treats each link like a vote of trust. More trusted votes = higher rankings. This is the hardest thing to fake and the biggest "moat" the big agencies have. |
| **DR / Authority Score** | A 0–100 trust score (Ahrefs calls it DR, Semrush calls it Authority Score) built mostly from backlinks. Higher = easier to rank for everything. |

**One sentence for the sales team:** the agencies below get hundreds of free leads a month because Google sends them traffic; this report shows where they're strong, where they're soft, and the exact gaps we can walk through.

> **Important honesty note on the numbers.** We do **not** have a paid Ahrefs/Semrush subscription wired in. Traffic and keyword counts here are pulled from public sources (SimilarWeb, Semrush/Ahrefs free previews, third-party aggregators) and are labelled **High / Med / Low** confidence. They're accurate enough to make decisions; they are **not** exact. Before we spend ad money on specific keywords, we should confirm those in Google Keyword Planner. (This is also why building/buying a real keyword tool would help — separate conversation.)

## 1. Executive summary (read this if nothing else)

**Who we actually compete with in Google:** a mix of (a) two national giants — **Thrive Agency** and **WebFX**, (b) one fading restaurant specialist — **Gourmet Marketing**, and (c) a long tail of small regional agencies and directory/"top-10-list" sites.

**The three biggest takeaways:**

1. **The giants are unbeatable head-on, but they're beatable sideways.** We will not outrank WebFX for "local SEO services." We *can* beat everyone for "restaurant website design **Houston**" and hundreds of city + service combinations they don't bother to build well. Our existing 60 city pages are exactly the right weapon — we just need more of them and deeper content.

2. **The restaurant lane is wide open.** The most credentialed restaurant agency (Gourmet Marketing) has quietly pivoted to **hotels** — its blog and case studies are now mostly hotels. The pure restaurant specialists that remain (TheDigitalRestaurant, LocalRestaurantSEO, etc.) are small and beatable. Restaurants are our proven proof + our easiest wins.

3. **Everyone wins with free tools and "what does it cost" pages — and we're under-using both.** WebFX's #1 traffic source is free tools (calculators, checkers). Thrive uses "best X companies" list articles. Both rank "how much does X cost" pages. We have one tool (the Review Calculator) and zero cost pages. This is the single clearest gap.

**What we do better than them already:** our city pages are genuinely customized (not boilerplate), our schema/technical SEO is clean, and our restaurant credibility is real. **What they do better than us:** scale of content, free tools, hard proof numbers ("$462M generated," exact % results), trust badges/awards, and "cost of X" pages.

## 3. Deep dives — what each rival does better, and where they're soft

### 3.1 Thrive Agency (thriveagency.com) — our most omnipresent rival
Arlington, TX. ~160 staff, founded 2005. Shows up in almost every search we care about, both its own pages and inside "best agency" lists.

**Traffic (High confidence):** ~200K–300K total visits/month, the majority from free Google traffic. ~1 million backlinks from ~14,000 sites — that's a serious trust moat. *However, their organic traffic dropped ~15% in May 2026 — a possible crack (likely Google's AI-search changes hurting their generic content).*

**What they do better than us:**
1. **Programmatic city × industry × service pages at scale** — e.g. "Dallas Restaurant SEO Company," "LA Restaurant Web Design Company." This is the exact engine we have, but they run it across dozens of cities. **This is the #1 tactic for us to copy and out-execute.**
2. **"Best [X] companies" list articles** (e.g. "12 Best SEO Companies for Restaurants 2026") — and they list themselves #1. Cheap to write, ranks for high-intent searches.
3. **Risk-reversal messaging everywhere:** "month-to-month, no contracts, 95% client retention." This kills the #1 objection a restaurant owner has (fear of being locked in). **The sales team should be saying this on calls.**
4. **Trust badges stacked:** Inc. 5000 seven years running, Google Premier Partner, 4.7★ on Clutch (100+ reviews).
5. **Branded tools** ("Thrive Score") as a hook.

**Where they're soft (our openings):**
- Their city pages are **thin and templated** — visitors leave in ~28 seconds. A genuinely local, specific page (real local screenshots, real local proof) beats them.
- **They're a generalist** — restaurants are 1 of 30+ industries on a template. We can out-specialize them with real restaurant depth.
- Only ~3 cities (Dallas, LA, Arlington) have their full restaurant page set. **We can blanket Texas restaurant + local terms before they get there.**
- Generic brand voice, no pricing, big-agency feel — a small local shop can promise owner-level attention they can't.

### 3.2 WebFX (webfx.com) — the playbook to study, not the fight to pick
30+ years, 750+ staff, DR 89. One of the best-ranking agency domains in the US.

**Traffic:** sources disagree (SimilarWeb ~400K total, an Ahrefs aggregator ~150K organic) — call it **hundreds of thousands of free visits/month** for ~18K+ keywords. Their trust score (DR 89, 33K linking sites) is **not something we can match** — it's 30 years of compounding.

**What they do better than everyone (and what we can copy at small scale):**
1. **Free tools as lead magnets — their single biggest move.** They host **28+ free tools** (ROAS calculator, readability checker, etc.). Their *highest-traffic pages are tools, not service pages.* Example: a single "random color generator" tool pulls ~14K visits/month. **We already started this with the Review Calculator — this confirms the strategy is right; we should add 2–3 more focused tools and lean in.**
2. **"How much does X cost?" pages** — e.g. "how much does SEO cost." High buying intent, low competition. **We have zero of these. Easiest gap to close.**
3. **"Learn" hubs + glossaries** — clusters of explainer pages that own definitional searches ("what is local SEO") and feed internal links.
4. **Original data content** — e.g. "we analyzed 2.3B sessions." Even a small version ("we audited 100 Houston restaurant websites") earns links cheaply.
5. **Partial pricing transparency** ("starting at $X/month") — rare in this industry, builds trust.

**What is NOT copyable:** their 30 years of backlinks, 750 staff, and custom software. **Lesson: don't try to out-*size* WebFX — copy the *mechanism* (free tool + tight topic cluster + cost pages) aimed at one niche.**

### 3.3 Gourmet Marketing (gourmetmarketing.net) — the restaurant specialist that left the room
The most credentialed "restaurant" agency in our set — but it has **largely pivoted to hotels** (its blog, 5 of 6 case studies, and headline stats are now hotel-focused). This is a strategic gift: the restaurant SMB lane it used to own is now under-served.

**Traffic (Med confidence):** only ~8,700 visits/month, ~256 keywords — and most of those are **branded** (people Googling "gourmet marketing"). Roughly a fifth of their traffic is overseas (job-seekers/curiosity, not buyers). **Translation: their actual buyer-intent restaurant rankings are weak and very beatable.**

**What they do better than us:**
1. **A "credibility wall" of hard numbers** above the fold: "$462M generated for partners," "17:1 average ad ROI," award counts. Concrete dollars build instant trust. **We show almost no hard result numbers — big gap.**
2. **Named premium client logos + attributed testimonials** (real name, title, company) — not anonymous quotes.
3. **Industry e-books as soft lead magnets** before the sales call.
4. **Clean service-page structure** (separate page for restaurant SEO, restaurant web design, restaurant branding, etc.).

**Where they're soft (our openings):**
- **They've left restaurants for hotels** — an attention gap in exactly our niche.
- **No city/local pages** — wide-open local opportunity.
- **Thin service pages** (feature lists, no method, no pricing, no per-client results), **only ~6 case studies**, and **no free audit/tool.** We can out-depth and out-tool them.

### 3.4 The real SERP rivals (the smaller players we actually outrank-or-lose-to)
When you actually Google our target terms, the page is mostly **small regional agencies + "top 10" list sites**, not the giants. That's good news.

**Recurring smaller rivals worth knowing by name:** TheDigitalRestaurant, LocalRestaurantSEO, RestaurantEngine (cheap template player), BlueTone Media, Flying V Group, Red Dash Media (NJ), and in Texas: Vorgestern (runs a smart city-page strategy worth studying), Web Sales Group, CZone Star, Hyperlinks Media, Red Spot Design.

**Directories that dominate page 1 (join them, don't fight them):** DesignRush, Clutch, HigherVisibility, FitSmallBusiness, OneLittleWeb. For terms like "restaurant website design company," page 1 is almost entirely these "best agencies" lists — so the play is to **get listed on them** and/or **publish our own comparison content**, not to fight for a pure service page.

## 5. Keyword opportunities — where we can actually win

> Volumes are estimates (label **E**); confirm in Keyword Planner before spending. The pattern is what matters: **lots of small, low-competition, local + "cost" searches beat a few giant head terms.**

### 5.1 Ironclad easy wins (low competition + clear buyer intent + we can rank)
These are the ones to do first.

**Restaurant lens:**
- `restaurant web design services`, `restaurant SEO services`, `restaurant SEO agency`, `local SEO for restaurants` — core money terms, the SERP favors agencies like us over the SaaS platforms.
- **The whole city set:** `restaurant website design {city}`, `restaurant SEO {city}`, `restaurant marketing {city}` across all 10 TX metros — individually small (~30–150 searches each) but low-KD and they stack. *This is our biggest asset; we already have the machine.*
- `how much does a restaurant website cost` — **verified real demand**, low competition, people searching "cost" are ready to buy.
- `Google Business Profile for restaurants`, `restaurant Google Ads management` — tiny volume, near-zero competition, pure buying intent.
- Blog easy wins (we've already written most): `how to get more restaurant reviews`, `how to respond to bad restaurant reviews`, `how to rank restaurant on Google`, `restaurant local SEO checklist`.

**Generic local-business lens (the pivot):**
- `affordable web design for small business`, `GBP optimization service`, `Google Ads management for small business`.
- City versions: `small business web design {city}`, `local SEO {city}`, `Google Business Profile optimization {city}`.
- The big untapped surface: **`{industry} web design {city}`** (dentist / lawyer / HVAC / salon / gym) — the generic twin of our restaurant engine. Pick 2–3 high-value verticals once the niche is decided.
- "Cost" pages again: `how much does local SEO cost`, `how much does a small business website cost`, `is Google Ads worth it for small business`.

### 5.2 Fight to avoid (for now)
Head terms owned by giants + directories: `local SEO services`, `local SEO agency`, `small business website design`, `restaurant branding agency`. Only approach these through long-tail or our own list/comparison content — not pure service pages.

## 6. The action plan — ranked, in plain language

Ordered by "fastest leads for least effort first."

1. **Publish the 11 restaurant blog drafts.** They're already written and target easy-win searches. This is free traffic sitting on the shelf. *(Needs: paste into the editor, add featured images, publish.)*
2. **Build 2–3 "How much does X cost" pages** (restaurant website cost, local SEO cost, Google Ads cost). Low effort, low competition, attracts ready-to-buy searchers. WebFX proves this works.

4. **Add hard proof numbers to the site.** Even modest real numbers ("X reviews generated," "Y% more calls") — copy Gourmet's credibility-wall idea. The sales team likely has client wins we're not showing.
5. **Add risk-reversal messaging** ("month-to-month, no lock-in, free strategy call") prominently — steal Thrive's best line. Costs nothing, removes the #1 objection.
6. **Build 1–2 more free micro-tools** beyond the Review Calculator (e.g. a free local-SEO/GBP audit). This is WebFX's #1 move and our clearest lead-magnet gap. *(Note: a full "website grader" was previously vetoed by Hasham — these would be smaller, focused tools, worth a quick re-check with him.)*
7. **Write 2–3 "best/comparison" articles** ("best restaurant website builders," "DIY vs agency local SEO") to catch decision-stage searchers.

9. **(After the niche decision)** Launch industry landing pages for the chosen 2nd vertical, then multiply by city — same playbook as restaurants.
10. **Long game:** start earning backlinks (local press, partner sites, the directories above). This is slow but it's the moat the giants have and we don't.
